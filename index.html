About Me

E2EE SecuritySPIES Lab · TAMU
13 Publications217 citations

I'm a PhD Candidate in Computer Science at Texas A&M University (GPA 4.0), working with Prof. Nitesh Saxena in the SPIES Lab. My research asks a simple, uncomfortable question: when end-to-end encryption meets real humans and real AI systems, where does trust actually break?

I design and run IRB-approved user studies, build attack and defense prototypes — from device-linking attacks with 90%+ success rates to Passkey-Anchored Linking (PAL), a FIDO2/WebAuthn defense — and develop LLM-based evaluation pipelines that probe what AI assistants can leak from encrypted conversations.

Before research, I spent four years shipping production software: OAuth2/SAML systems in Germany, a debit-card API serving 3M+ banking customers in Bangladesh, and secure ASP.NET platforms for 100K+ users.

News & Vision

What's happening in my research right now — and where all of it is heading.

My Vision

I want "end-to-end encrypted" to mean what people believe it means — even now that AI assistants sit inside our most private conversations.

The cryptography is strong; the breaks happen where humans, devices, and AI meet the protocol: authentication ceremonies people skip, QR codes that can be replayed, assistants that infer what they were never shown. My research attacks those seams first — then rebuilds them with defenses people can actually use, like hardware-backed passkeys and ceremony-bound group admission.

The goal is a trust layer for secure communication that survives contact with real users and real AI.

— Md Shahidur Rahaman
